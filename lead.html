02 — Context

One Designer. One ERP. One Unicorn.

LEAD School was a unicorn — an EdTech company that had cracked the code on bringing quality education infrastructure to schools across India at scale. Somebody had to design the part that made school owners actually buy in.

I was the solo designer on Nucleus — LEAD's B2B ERP system and the academic management platform that school owners, principals, and coordinators used to run their entire school. Attendance patterns. Academic performance. Teacher effectiveness. ELGA allocation. Subject mapping. Operational health. All of it, in one interface.

This wasn't a consumer product where users could tolerate friction. This was a B2B platform where confusion at the interface level translated directly into lost school partnerships. If a principal couldn't understand their dashboard, LEAD lost a school. The stakes were that direct and that high.

And I owned it alone. What I didn't own alone was research — because across the full design organization (7 designers, 4 products, B2B and B2C) I became the person everyone relied on for research methodology, heuristic evaluation, and usability testing. Not because I was assigned to it. Because I understood that good design across any product starts with the same foundation.

"I wasn't just designing a product. I was building the design capability of an entire organization while shipping my own work simultaneously."

03 — Research Leadership

The Research Everyone Relied On

40+ interviews, 20+ usability tests, and the insights that changed four products.

The research program I led covered the full product ecosystem. User interviews with school owners evaluating whether to renew their LEAD partnership. Usability testing with principals using the platform daily. Heuristic evaluations of teacher and student portals that other designers owned.

The 58-page research report delivered live to the entire product team wasn't just documentation — it was the strategic foundation for the next quarter's roadmap across every product LEAD designed.

04 — AI Design

Yiso — Designing AI Before It Had a Name

2022 · The AI recommendation engine nobody knew was ahead of its time.

In 2022 I designed an AI recommendation engine called Yiso. At the time nobody called it AI. The product team called it an "automated recommendation tool." I called it a solution to a research-validated problem.

ELGA — English Language and General Awareness — was LEAD's flagship academic program and the primary reason schools signed partnership agreements. Getting ELGA right during onboarding was critical. Getting it wrong meant errors cascading through the entire academic year.

70% of LEAD's customer support queries were about ELGA allocation errors.

The existing process required principals to manually understand complex backend dependencies — which class configurations mapped to which ELGA levels, which division structures triggered which student groupings, how individual student marks affected allocation. A multi-variable logic problem dressed up as a school admin task.

Yiso changed the entire interaction model. Instead of asking principals to understand the system logic, Yiso did the reasoning for them. Three decisions — ELGA level confirmation, division name, student name. Everything else automated. Dependencies detected. Configurations validated. Recommended allocation surfaced with clear reasoning.

I designed the full interaction model — the chatbot personality, contextual guidance at every step, auto-detection logic interface, multichannel data entry options, and 6+ use case states for different onboarding scenarios.

"I didn't design an AI feature. I designed away a problem that was costing the business real money and real relationships — and the solution happened to use intelligence."

05 — High-Stakes Delivery

The 48-Hour Moment That Proved Everything

There's a moment in every designer's career that tells you exactly where you stand in the organization's trust hierarchy. Mine came at 9pm.

LEAD needed a proof of concept for the OAC Report — a mobile-first observation and coaching report tool for Area Excellence Managers who visited schools and needed to document teacher observations, classroom feedback, and improvement action plans in the field.

The existing process used Google Docs and PDFs. 3–4 hours per report. AEMs were burning out. Turnover was high. The stakeholder meeting was in 48 hours. They needed a validated POC. And they chose me.

Not because I was available. Because I was trusted.

In 48 hours I delivered a complete mobile-first interaction model — report creation, teacher observation documentation, overall school feedback, picworthy moment capture, progressive disclosure for field use, auto-selection logic, draft state management, and a new design system component library aligned with LEAD's marketing guidelines.

The stakeholder meeting happened. The POC was validated. The project was greenlit. Task completion time when it shipped: 10 minutes. Down from 3–4 hours. 96% reduction.

The team won the Best Group Award. I was part of that team.

"Speed without quality is just chaos. Quality without speed is just theory. The 48-hour POC was both — and that's why they called me."

08 — Reflection

What Design Leadership Without the Title Looks Like

I never had a design lead title at LEAD. I was a Product Designer — singular, individual contributor, no direct reports. But here's what I actually did.

I ran the research program for an entire design organization. I mentored junior designers on UX methodology and interaction design principles. I set the quality standard the team measured itself against. I was trusted with the most urgent, highest-stakes deliverable when the organization needed it most. When my workload became too heavy they hired another designer — not to replace me, but to support me.

That's leadership. It just didn't have a title attached to it.

What I'd do differently: Formalise the mentorship earlier. The design quality improvements I drove through informal guidance could have been accelerated with structured design critiques, documented research frameworks, and explicit quality standards.

What this project proves: That I can own a complex B2B enterprise product end to end. That I can lead research at organizational scale. That I can build AI-powered interaction models before AI was an expectation. That I can deliver under extreme time pressure without sacrificing quality. That I can make the people around me better designers.

"They didn't give me the title. They gave me the trust. In the end, that's what leadership actually is."
