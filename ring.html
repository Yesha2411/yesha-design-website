02 — The Brief

2 Million Users. One Flow Nobody Had Mapped.

The disbursement flow was generating a disproportionate number of ops tickets. Users were getting stuck. The team knew something was wrong. Nobody had mapped exactly what.

The disbursement flow — the critical moment when a user applies for a Power Loan and money actually reaches their bank account — was the breaking point. My brief was technically about transaction flows and bank aggregator integrations.

What I actually walked into was a product that had grown screen by screen without a consistent design system holding it together. And the cracks were showing exactly where users could least afford them — at the moment of financial commitment.

"The disbursement flow wasn't just a UX problem. It was a trust problem. At the moment a user is about to receive money, any confusion, any inconsistency, any dead end — they bail. And when they bail, the business loses the user."

03 — Persona Research

Who Was Actually Failing

Four archetypes. Wildly different mental models of money. The existing personas were thin — I redefined them across four user types, each with distinct financial literacy, tech comfort, and tolerance for process friction.

Neha Gupta, 27
Salaried · Call centre · First-time borrower. Needs guidance through every stage. Fears fraud. Doesn't understand NACH or KYC. Wants flexibility in repayment dates and no hidden fees.
Digitally anxious
Ashwin Patel, 38

Transparency-hungry
Varsha Pandya, 22
Freelancer · Gen Z · Lifestyle borrower. Zero paperwork expectation. Wants instant disbursal and automated payments. 15-day repayment cycle feels too short. Loyalty perks matter.
Zero-friction expectation
Hemkant Rao, 58
Retired · Pension income · Low-tech. Needs co-borrower support. Frustrated by long KYC processes. Wants consolidated loan tracking and terms explained in plain language.
Trust-first user

What this mapping revealed wasn't just four different users — it was four completely different definitions of "this process makes sense." What feels transparent to Ashwin is overwhelming to Neha. What feels fast to Varsha is reckless to Hemkant. A single redesign had to hold all of them.

04 — Competitive Benchmarking

The Competitive Lens

I led three designers across six products to find what Ring was missing — not to copy what competitors did well, but to expose the gap between user expectations and what Ring was delivering.

I organized a structured competitive benchmarking exercise. I assigned three designers to different workflow tracks and mapped how competing fintech products handled the same critical moments:

Loan application flows KYC patterns Disbursement states Error handling Account management Transaction history

The finding that kept surfacing: every competitor had clearer disbursement state communication. Ring left users in the dark at the exact moment they most needed to know what was happening with their money.

"The competitive analysis didn't just show us what to fix. It gave us the language to prioritize it. 'Everyone else tells users where their money is' is a harder argument to ignore than 'our disbursement states need work.'"

05 — Heuristic Evaluation

What the Audit Revealed

42 issues. 11 critical. Consistency was the silent killer — the product was actively breaking users' mental models by showing the same information differently depending on how they navigated to it.

06 — The Redesign

Redesigning the Critical Path

35+ screens. One goal: money reaches the user without confusion. The disbursement flow was identified as highest-priority based on the combined signal from persona research, competitive analysis, and heuristic findings.

The redesign covered the full Power Loan application journey — every screen, every state:

Offer entry + promotion 3-step profile completion PAN + Aadhaar KYC Video KYC handoff Loan amount + tenure Bank details + auto-debit KFS + T&C acceptance Disbursement states ×4 Error + rejection states

Every design decision anchored to the personas. For Neha: clear progress indicators, plain-language consent copy. For Ashwin: full fee breakdown upfront before commitment. For Varsha: momentum-preserving transitions, no unnecessary gates. For Hemkant: trust signals at every bank handoff, reassurance copy during processing waits.

"The disbursement 'on hold' state was a blank wall in the original design. Users had no idea if something had gone wrong. The redesign gave them a status, a next action, and a contact path. That single screen change reduced a significant category of support tickets."

07 — Business Impact

The Roadmap Shift

Research findings didn't just inform design. They changed what got built next.

The combined output of persona research, competitive benchmarking, and heuristic evaluation gave the product team something they hadn't had before: a ranked, evidence-backed list of what to fix and in what order.

The disbursement flow moved to the top of the roadmap — not because a designer said it should, but because the data made the case. Two specific product areas — disbursement state communication and the Add Bank consistency fix — became Q3 priorities directly traceable to audit findings.

08 — Reflection

What It Proved

You don't redesign screens. You diagnose what's costing the business.

Ring was my first time pairing a full heuristic evaluation with competitive benchmarking on a product already at scale. The lesson that stuck: at 2M users, a UI inconsistency isn't an aesthetic problem — it's a trust problem that compounds across millions of interactions.

Every time a user hit the inconsistent Add Bank screen, they had to relearn. Every time a disbursement sat silently on hold, a support ticket was filed and a user's confidence eroded.

What I'm most proud of isn't the screens I redesigned. It's that I came in, audited the whole product, surfaced the pattern nobody had named, and gave the team a prioritization framework they could act on. The design work followed the research. That sequence — understand the system before touching the interface — is something I apply to every engagement now.

The four personas I redefined are still the archetypes the team references. The heuristic findings directly shaped what shipped next. Five months. Real impact.
